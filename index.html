02 · The near-term plan & the waterline
What it is
The near-term (~4-week) plan. Committed work sits above the waterline; the backlog sits below.
How it works
The engine ranks every ready job by how critical it is and fills the crew's capacity down to the line. A critical job that isn't ready stays below — and its cost of putting it off comes from a prediction of the downtime it would risk. The one place a real predictive model does work.
What value it provides
Priority is not the same as sequence. The planner sees the whole plan and exactly what's deferred and why — the cost of leaving a critical job undone is visible, not buried.

03 · Planning at scale
What it is
The long-term (~6-month) view as a grid the planner triages at scale — with both horizons and a Board / List toggle.
How it works
The same critical / ready scores place every dot. The engine groups related jobs into clusters and builds the plan under crew and time-window limits. This far out, most critical work isn't ready — so the job is to move it rightward into ready before it fails.
What value it provides
Read the weight and shape of the whole backlog at a glance. A planner handles tens to hundreds of jobs, not ten. Flip to the view the moment needs — grid far out, list near-term.

04 · The board explains itself
What it is
Before the planner touches anything, the board shows its reasoning — one explanation per job.
How it works
Generative AI turns the engine's decisions — the scores, the crew and window limits, the downtime prediction — into plain-language explanations, one per job, on demand.
What value it provides
A tool you can trust and argue with, not a black box. Accept the first pass — or push back, with the reasons right in front of you.

05 · Recalibration
What it is
Adjusting the board and the backlog — the core move.
How it works
Pull a job up and the engine re-solves against crew capacity and suggests what to drop — the jobs whose delay costs least. Push one down and it moves to the right horizon. Drag one in from the backlog and it lands in the right place on its own. Every move is re-explained.
What value it provides
The planner keeps the judgment; the engine does the capacity math and shows the consequence — one move, fully re-explained.

06 · The trade-off
What it is
The one number the board optimizes — the trade between planned downtime now and unplanned risk later.
How it works
Planned sums the service hours of the committed jobs. Deferred risk sums, for each critical job left below the line, the downtime it would cause if it failed × how likely it is to fail this window.
What value it provides
One score makes the trade explicit — a few hours of work now against the risk sitting in what you defer. The planner sees the cost of waiting.

07 · The outputs — ledger & handoff
What it is
When the plan is locked, every decision is written to a log with its reason — and the board generates the scheduler's handoff.
How it works
The log records each decision with a short auto-written summary. Generative AI drafts the handoff from the locked plan. That log is also the seed for proof — when a job that got put off later fails, the log traces the downtime back to the decision.
What value it provides
Decisions can be proven out later — this is how the loop actually closes — and the handoff to the crew is clean.
